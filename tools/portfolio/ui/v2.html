What you kept of the available gain

Your own deposits are stripped out of this, because nobody gets credit for their own money. What is left is the gain — what your strategy earned on it, against what the same contributions and withdrawals would have earned in a passive reference over the same months. The fee is separated from the rest, because “your fee explains this much” is a different claim from “your strategy explains the rest”.

Measured against

One at a time. The available gain is defined against a single reference — there is no such thing as the gain available across three strategies at once — so this picks one. Every row says what it is, and flags a caution where there is one, before you choose it.

      Read this alongside it

      • This is not investment advice. It makes no recommendation and says nothing about what you should do next.
      • A reference strategy is a yardstick, not a menu. Nothing here says that fund was available to you, in your plan or at that price. It is what the same money in the same months would have done somewhere else.
      • The result is historical and benchmark-dependent. A different reference strategy, or a different period, gives a different answer — switch the reference in the list and watch every figure on this page move.
      • A short period is a small sample. Five years containing one bear market is thin evidence about anything.
      • Returns aren't the whole job. A fee may also buy tax work, planning, or the coaching that stopped you selling at the bottom. This measures none of that.
